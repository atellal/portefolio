Micro-optical structures fabricated by two-photon polymerisation — PHENOMENON (EU)
Project · EN

The PHENOMENON Project

Laser manufacturing of 3D nanostructured optics using advanced photochemistry.

Laser Manufacturing of 3D Nanostructured Optics using Advanced Photochemistry — a European project carried out at the ENS de Lyon (CNRS).

Objective & context

The PHENOMENON project explored innovative methods to fabricate 3D nanostructured optics by combining laser-based manufacturing and advanced photochemistry. The aim was to design and realise diffractive micro- and nanostructures with tailored optical functionalities — focusing, anti-reflection, beam shaping — using holographic lithography and two-photon polymerisation.

My role & contributions

  • Design & simulation of diffractive micro-optics — optimised periodic nanostructures for light management using FEM (COMSOL).
  • Holographic lithography — phase-mask calculations and SLM-based projection systems to fabricate 2D and 3D structures.
  • Modelling two-photon polymerisation — simulated photo-polymerisation dynamics to predict resolution limits and optimise parameters.
  • Experimental validation — correlated simulation results with SEM and optical characterisation.

Outcomes & impact

  • Fabricated diffractive micro-optics with sub-wavelength resolution,
  • Improved understanding of fabrication tolerances in holography and 2PP,
  • Strengthened the simulation ↔ experiment feedback loop,
  • Contributed to joint publications and dissemination.
Reflection

The project highlighted the gap between ideal simulations and experimental realities — material response, tolerances, alignment — and showed the power of interdisciplinary collaboration across optics, chemistry and materials science.
